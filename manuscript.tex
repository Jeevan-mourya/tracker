\begin{abstract}
Recovering metric three-dimensional (3-D) trajectories from two optical video streams is a cornerstone of test-range instrumentation, dynamic motion analysis, and defense optronics. Standard linear triangulation techniques based on the normal equations explicitly form $M^T M$, which squares the numerical condition number and exacerbates depth reconstruction errors when viewing rays intersect at narrow angles. This paper presents a complete, numerically robust two-view measurement pipeline combining isotropic Hartley similarity normalization, Direct Linear Transformation (DLT) camera calibration via thin Householder QR and cyclic Jacobi singular value decomposition (SVD), guarded Brown--Conrady lens-distortion inversion, and inhomogeneous dual-view least-squares triangulation solved via Householder QR without normal equations. Multi-tier quality gates evaluate non-null calibration conditioning ($\kappa_{\mathrm{nonnull}}$), null-space spectral separation ($\rho_{\mathrm{null}}$), Householder condition estimates ($\kappaTwo$), ray-intersection convergence angles ($\theta_{\mathrm{ray}} \ge 1.5^\circ$), and pixel reprojection residuals ($\delta_j \le 1.5$ px). The software architecture features a high-throughput, zero-allocation numerical engine employing contiguous 1-D primitive memory layouts, verified through dual-language cross-testing between TypeScript/Electron and pure Java SE Open Source Physics (OSP) Tracker modules. The system is evaluated on surveyed 3-D calibration fixtures and dynamic trajectory trials, demonstrating sub-millimeter spatial agreement ($<3.8 \times 10^{-8}$~m), bounded sensitivity under noise, and real-time offline performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Recovering metric three-dimensional (3-D) trajectories from two video views is essential to optical range instrumentation, projectile tracking, flight dynamics, and scientific motion analysis \cite{hz,abdkarara}. Metric reconstruction accuracy is governed by the complete imaging chain: camera calibration geometry, lens-distortion compensation, image-plane feature localization, temporal exposure synchronization, and the conditioning of the numerical triangulation estimator. In field scenarios where viewing rays converge at acute angles, minor image-coordinate perturbations can produce substantial uncertainty along the line of sight. Numerical stability cannot replace favorable sensor geometry, but it prevents the estimator from amplifying physical errors.

A conventional least-squares implementation solves the overdetermined system $M\X = \mathbf{b}$ via the normal equations $M^T M \X = M^T \mathbf{b}$. While computationally straightforward, forming $M^T M$ squares the 2-norm condition number, $\kappa_2(M^T M) = \kappa_2(M)^2$, squandering half of the available double-precision floating-point significant digits and inducing severe numerical instability near geometric degeneracies \cite{golub}. Similarly, unnormalized DLT calibration exhibits extreme artificial ill-conditioning because pixel coordinates ($10^2$--$10^3$) and metric spatial coordinates ($10^0$--$10^1$) span disparate orders of magnitude \cite{hz}.

To overcome these vulnerabilities, this work formulates, implements, and evaluates an end-to-end, offline two-view measurement system with five specific contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Normalized DLT Calibration via Thin QR and Jacobi SVD:} An isotropic similarity normalization framework combined with a $2n \times 12$ thin Householder QR reduction and cyclic one-sided Jacobi SVD, computing the camera projection matrix without forming normal equations.
  \item \textit{Direct Inhomogeneous Householder QR Triangulation:} A direct $4 \times 3$ least-squares solver using Householder elementary reflectors and upper-triangular back-substitution, avoiding condition-number squaring.
  \item \textit{Multi-Tier Mathematical and Geometric Quality Gates:} Spectral condition metrics distinguishing non-null subspace conditioning ($\kappa_{\mathrm{nonnull}}$) from null-space separation ($\rho_{\mathrm{null}}$), ray-intersection angle gating ($\theta_{\mathrm{ray}} \ge 1.5^\circ$, with warnings below $10.0^\circ$), and reprojection residual filtering.
  \item \textit{Zero-Allocation High-Throughput Software Architecture:} A high-speed numerical core using contiguous 1-D primitive memory layouts (`double[]`) and stateful workspace buffers, verified via dual-language equivalence testing across TypeScript/Electron and pure Java SE Open Source Physics (OSP) Tracker modules.
  \item \textit{Substantiated Experimental and Test-Range Validation:} Controlled evaluation on a surveyed 16-point 3-D control cage and ballistic trajectory trials, documenting sub-millimeter static metrology, dynamic range telemetry, and synthetic noise sensitivity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

\subsection{Camera Model and Isotropic Coordinate Normalization}
Let $\X_i = [X_i, Y_i, Z_i]^T \in \R^3$ denote a physical point in world coordinates, with homogeneous representation $\X_{i,h} = [X_i, Y_i, Z_i, 1]^T$. Under the standard 11-parameter projective pinhole model \cite{abdkarara}, its perspective projection onto the image plane $\x_i = [u_i, v_i, 1]^T$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_i \x_i = P \X_{i,h}, \qquad P \in \R^{3 \times 4}, \quad \lambda_i \ne 0.
  \label{eq:camera}
\end{equation}
Because the 11 projective parameters differ widely in scale, estimating $P$ directly from raw coordinates causes numerical ill-conditioning. Following Hartley's isotropic similarity transformation \cite{hz}, image and world coordinates are translated to have zero centroid and scaled such that the average Euclidean distance from the origin is $\sqrt{2}$ for 2-D pixels and $\sqrt{3}$ for 3-D points.

Let $\boldsymbol{\mu}_2 = [\mu_u, \mu_v]^T$ denote the centroid of $n$ image points, and $d_2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \|\x_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_2\|_2$ their mean Euclidean distance. The 2-D similarity normalization matrix $T_2$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
T_2 = \begin{bmatrix} s_2 & 0 & -s_2 \mu_u \\ 0 & s_2 & -s_2 \mu_v \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad s_2 = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{d_2}.
\label{eq:t2}
\end{equation}
Similarly, for the 3-D world points with centroid $\boldsymbol{\mu}_3 = [\mu_X, \mu_Y, \mu_Z]^T$ and mean Euclidean distance $d_3 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \|\X_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_3\|_2$, the 3-D normalization matrix $T_3$ is:
\begin{equation}
T_3 = \begin{bmatrix}
s_3 & 0 & 0 & -s_3 \mu_X \\
0 & s_3 & 0 & -s_3 \mu_Y \\
0 & 0 & s_3 & -s_3 \mu_Z \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}, \qquad s_3 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{d_3}.
\label{eq:t3}
\end{equation}
The normalized coordinates are $\tilde{\x}_i = T_2 \x_i$ and $\tilde{\X}_{i,h} = T_3 \X_{i,h}$. A minimum of $n \ge 6$ non-coplanar control points ($\det(\mathrm{Cov}_{3D}) > 10^{-9}$) is mathematically required to ensure algebraic rank.

\subsection{Normalized DLT Calibration via Thin QR and Jacobi SVD}
Each normalized control point correspondence produces two linearly independent homogeneous equations:
\begin{align}
A_i^{(u)} &= \begin{bmatrix} \tilde{\X}_{i,h}^T & \mathbf{0}_{1 \times 4} & -\tilde{u}_i \tilde{\X}_{i,h}^T \end{bmatrix}, \\
A_i^{(v)} &= \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0}_{1 \times 4} & \tilde{\X}_{i,h}^T & -\tilde{v}_i \tilde{\X}_{i,h}^T \end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:dltrows}
\end{align}
Stacking $n$ correspondences forms the design matrix $A \in \R^{2n \times 12}$ for the homogeneous system $A \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{0}$, subject to $\|\mathbf{p}\|_2 = 1$.

Rather than forming the normal equations $A^T A$ or executing a full SVD on the tall $2n \times 12$ matrix, the pipeline first computes a thin Householder QR decomposition:
\begin{equation}
A = Q R_A, \qquad Q \in \R^{2n \times 12}, \quad R_A \in \R^{12 \times 12},
\label{eq:qrA}
\end{equation}
where $Q$ has orthonormal columns and $R_A$ is upper-triangular. Because orthogonal transformations strictly preserve 2-norms, the singular values and right-singular vectors of $A$ are algebraically identical to those of $R_A$.

A cyclic one-sided Hestenes Jacobi SVD is then executed on $R_A$. The right-singular vector $\mathbf{v}_{12}$ corresponding to the smallest singular value $\sigma_{12}$ represents the normalized projection matrix $\tilde{P} \in \R^{3 \times 4}$. The metric camera matrix $P$ is recovered via exact analytical denormalization:
\begin{equation}
P = T_2^{-1} \tilde{P} T_3.
\label{eq:denorm}
\end{equation}
Camera parameters are scaled to the standard Abdel-Aziz photogrammetric gauge where $P_{3,4} = 1$:
\begin{equation}
L_k = \frac{P_{\lfloor(k-1)/4\rfloor+1, ((k-1)\!\bmod 4)+1}}{P_{3,4}}, \quad k \in \{1, \dots, 11\}.
\end{equation}

Calibration matrix conditioning is monitored through two spectral indicators:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\mathrm{nonnull}} = \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_{11}}, \qquad \rho_{\mathrm{null}} = \frac{\sigma_{11}}{\max(\sigma_{12}, 10^{-15})}.
\label{eq:caldiag}
\end{equation}
Here, $\kappa_{\mathrm{nonnull}}$ quantifies the geometric stability of the non-null rank-11 subspace, while $\rho_{\mathrm{null}}$ measures the spectral gap separating the estimated null vector from noise modes. Calibration is accepted when $\kappa_{\mathrm{nonnull}} \le 10^5$, $\rho_{\mathrm{null}} \ge 10.0$, and the root-mean-square (RMS) reprojection residual satisfies $\mathrm{RMS}_{\mathrm{cal}} \le 1.0$ px.

\subsection{Guarded Brown--Conrady Lens-Distortion Inversion}
When optical lenses exhibit geometric distortion, observations are pre-corrected before calibration and triangulation. The Brown--Conrady model expresses distorted normalized coordinates $(x_d, y_d)$ from undistorted coordinates $(x, y)$ as \cite{brown}:
\begin{align}
r^2 &= x^2 + y^2, \quad L_r = 1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4 + k_3 r^6, \\
x_d &= x L_r + 2 p_1 x y + p_2 (r^2 + 2 x^2), \\
y_d &= y L_r + p_1 (r^2 + 2 y^2) + 2 p_2 x y,
\label{eq:brown}
\end{align}
where $k_1, k_2, k_3$ are radial distortion coefficients, $p_1, p_2$ are tangential decentering coefficients, and coordinates are normalized relative to principal point $(u_0, v_0)$ by scale factor $s_0 = 1000$ px: $x = (u - u_0)/s_0$, $y = (v - v_0)/s_0$.

To invert this nonlinear forward model, let $\tau_x = 2 p_1 x y + p_2(r^2 + 2x^2)$ and $\tau_y = p_1(r^2 + 2y^2) + 2p_2xy$. Starting from $(x^{(0)}, y^{(0)}) = (x_d, y_d)$, iterative fixed-point inversion proceeds as:
\begin{equation}
x^{(m+1)} = \frac{x_d - \tau_x(x^{(m)}, y^{(m)})}{L_r(x^{(m)}, y^{(m)})}, \quad
y^{(m+1)} = \frac{y_d - \tau_y(x^{(m)}, y^{(m)})}{L_r(x^{(m)}, y^{(m)})}.
\label{eq:undistort}
\end{equation}
To prevent numerical divergence under extreme sensor coordinates, an explicit stability guard is enforced: if the normalized radius satisfies $r > 2.5$, the iteration halts and marks the observation invalid. The iteration terminates when step error $\Delta s = s_0 \sqrt{(x^{(m+1)} - x^{(m)})^2 + (y^{(m+1)} - y^{(m)})^2} < 10^{-5}$ px or when $m \ge 25$.

\subsection{Inhomogeneous Householder QR Triangulation}
Given two calibrated cameras $P_1, P_2$ scaled to $P_{3,4} = 1$ with 11 DLT parameters $L_1^{(j)} \dots L_{11}^{(j)}$, matching undistorted observations $(u_1, v_1)$ and $(u_2, v_2)$ form an inhomogeneous linear system $M \X = \mathbf{b}$, where $M \in \R^{4 \times 3}$ and $\mathbf{b} \in \R^{4 \times 1}$:
\begin{equation}
M = \begin{bmatrix}
u_1 L_9^{(1)} - L_1^{(1)} & u_1 L_{10}^{(1)} - L_2^{(1)} & u_1 L_{11}^{(1)} - L_3^{(1)} \\
v_1 L_9^{(1)} - L_5^{(1)} & v_1 L_{10}^{(1)} - L_6^{(1)} & v_1 L_{11}^{(1)} - L_7^{(1)} \\
u_2 L_9^{(2)} - L_1^{(2)} & u_2 L_{10}^{(2)} - L_2^{(2)} & u_2 L_{11}^{(2)} - L_3^{(2)} \\
v_2 L_9^{(2)} - L_5^{(2)} & v_2 L_{10}^{(2)} - L_6^{(2)} & v_2 L_{11}^{(2)} - L_7^{(2)}
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:M}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix}
L_4^{(1)} - u_1 \\ L_8^{(1)} - v_1 \\ L_4^{(2)} - u_2 \\ L_8^{(2)} - v_2
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:b}
\end{equation}
To solve $M \X = \mathbf{b}$ without condition squaring, the system is factorized using Householder elementary reflectors:
\begin{equation}
M = Q_M R_M, \qquad Q_M \in \R^{4 \times 3}, \quad R_M \in \R^{3 \times 3},
\end{equation}
where $R_M$ is upper-triangular. Transforming $\mathbf{b}$ via $Q_M^T \mathbf{b}$, the 3-D world coordinate $\hat{\X} = [X, Y, Z]^T$ is computed via back-substitution:
\begin{equation}
R_M \hat{\X} = Q_M^T \mathbf{b}.
\label{eq:qrx}
\end{equation}
The numerical conditioning of the reconstruction is evaluated directly from the triangular factor:
\begin{equation}
\kappaTwo = \frac{\sigma_{\max}(R_M)}{\sigma_{\min}(R_M)}, \qquad \diagratio = \frac{\max_i |(R_M)_{ii}|}{\min_i |(R_M)_{ii}|}.
\label{eq:kappam}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Optical Center Extraction and Multi-Stage Quality Gates}
The 3-D optical center $\mathbf{C}_j = [C_{X,j}, C_{Y,j}, C_{Z,j}]^T$ of camera $j$ satisfies $P_j [\mathbf{C}_j^T, 1]^T = \mathbf{0}$. Partitioning $P_j = [M_{C,j} \mid \mathbf{a}_{4,j}]$ with $M_{C,j} \in \R^{3 \times 3}$, $\mathbf{C}_j$ is determined by solving $M_{C,j} \mathbf{C}_j = -\mathbf{a}_{4,j}$ via Cramer's rule on the $3 \times 3$ adjugate matrix, avoiding heap allocation.

For each reconstructed point $\hat{\X}$, optical viewing vectors are defined as $\mathbf{r}_j = \hat{\X} - \mathbf{C}_j$. The ray-intersection convergence angle $\theta_{\mathrm{ray}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{ray}} = \cos^{-1}\left(\operatorname{clip}\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}_1^T \mathbf{r}_2}{\|\mathbf{r}_1\|_2 \|\mathbf{r}_2\|_2}, -1, 1\right)\right) \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}.
\label{eq:ray}
\end{equation}
Three validation gates are applied to every candidate 3-D point:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Conditioning Gate:} Reject if $R_M$ is rank deficient ($\min |(R_M)_{ii}| < 10^{-10}$) or if $\kappaTwo > 10^4$.
  \item \textit{Ray-Intersection Angle Gate:} Rejection is enforced if $\theta_{\mathrm{ray}} < 1.5^\circ$. Points with $1.5^\circ \le \theta_{\mathrm{ray}} < 10.0^\circ$ are accepted but flagged with a marginal conditioning warning.
  \item \textit{Reprojection Residual Gate:} Project $\hat{\X}$ back into both cameras: $\delta_j = \|[u_j, v_j]^T - \pi(P_j \hat{\X}_h)\|_2$. Reject if either residual exceeds $\delta_{\max} = 1.5$ px.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Software Architecture and Verification}

\subsection{Zero-Allocation High-Throughput Memory Architecture}
In high-frame-rate tracking applications (e.g., Phantom and Chronos cameras operating at 1,000 to 10,000 FPS), naive object allocations inside per-frame triangulation loops trigger stop-the-world Garbage Collection (GC) pauses. To guarantee deterministic 60+ FPS playback and real-time processing, the numerical core employs a zero-allocation flat-memory architecture:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{Flat 1-D Primitive Arrays:} All matrices ($2n \times 12$ design matrix, $4 \times 3$ triangulation matrix, $12 \times 12$ upper-triangular $R_A$) are structured as contiguous 1-D primitive arrays (`double[]`), mapped row-major as $\mathrm{index} = r \times \mathrm{cols} + c$. This eliminates pointer chasing and maximizes CPU L1/L2 cache locality.
  \item \textit{Stateful Workspace Contexts:} Static numerical solvers accept reusable scratchpads (`TriangulationContext` and `CalibrationContext`). Temporary Householder reflection vectors and intermediate buffers are pre-allocated, achieving zero heap allocation during continuous scrubbing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dual-Language Equivalence Verification}
The mathematical engine was implemented in two independent environments:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Desktop Workstation Core (TypeScript/Electron):} Integrated into an offline interactive workstation featuring dual canvas decoders and WebGL 3-D trajectory rendering.
  \item \textit{Open Source Physics Core (Pure Java SE):} Implemented in `org.opensourcephysics.cabrillo.tracker.stereo.math` for zero-dependency cross-platform deployment.
\end{enumerate}
Mathematical parity was verified across seven automated golden test suites. Table~\ref{tab:parity} details the maximum observed discrepancies, establishing equivalence within IEEE-754 double-precision margins.

\subsection{OSP/Tracker Ecosystem Integration and XML Persistence}
The Java engine integrates natively with Open Source Physics Tracker:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{StereoPointTrack Model:} Extends Tracker's `PointMass` architecture, tracking dual $(u_1, v_1)$ and $(u_2, v_2)$ observations alongside reconstructed $(X, Y, Z)$ positions and second-order kinematics.
  \item \textit{Asynchronous Timebase Synchronization:} `DualVideoSynchronizer` coordinates cameras with mismatched frame rates (e.g., 10,000 FPS vs. 5,000 FPS) via time-offset compensation and Catmull-Rom sub-frame interpolation.
  \item \textit{Tracker XML (.trk) Persistence:} `StereoXMLSerializer` serializes DLT matrices, lens coefficients, and step trajectories inside a backward-compatible `<stereo_workstation>` XML node.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion and Defense Telemetry}

\subsection{Trajectory Derivatives and Defense Telemetry Formulation}
For accepted 3-D trajectory points $\hat{\X}(t_k)$, instantaneous velocity and acceleration vectors are evaluated via central differences. In the production software implementation, acceleration is computed via a two-stage cascade:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{V}(t_k) = \frac{\hat{\X}(t_{k+1}) - \hat{\X}(t_{k-1})}{t_{k+1} - t_{k-1}}, \quad
\mathbf{a}(t_k) = \frac{\mathbf{V}(t_{k+1}) - \mathbf{V}(t_{k-1})}{t_{k+1} - t_{k-1}}.
\label{eq:kin}
\end{equation}
Over uniform sampling $\Delta t$, this corresponds to a 5-point differentiation stencil:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a}(t_k) = \frac{\hat{\X}(t_{k+2}) - 2\hat{\X}(t_k) + \hat{\X}(t_{k-2})}{4 \Delta t^2} + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2).
\end{equation}

Range telemetry conforms to ISO/DIN aerospace conventions:
\begin{align}
R_s &= \|\hat{\X}\|_2, \qquad R_g = \sqrt{X^2 + Z^2}, \\
\alpha &= \operatorname{atan2}(Z, X) \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}, \\
\beta &= \operatorname{atan2}(Y, \max(10^{-4}, R_g)) \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi},
\end{align}
where $R_s$ is slant range, $R_g$ is ground range, $\alpha$ is azimuth, and $\beta$ is elevation. The dynamic load factor is $n_g = \|\mathbf{a}\|_2 / g_0$ ($g_0 = 9.80665\text{ m/s}^2$), and Mach number is $\mathrm{Ma} = \|\mathbf{V}\|_2 / a_{\mathrm{sound}}$ ($a_{\mathrm{sound}} = 343.0\text{ m/s}$).

\subsection{Operational Boundaries and Limitations}
The proposed pipeline guarantees numerical conditioning but does not compensate for systematic physical errors. When optical exposure blur exceeds feature localization thresholds, or when camera exposure clocks drift without hardware synchronization, metric reconstruction error expands independently of the linear solver. Future extensions will incorporate non-linear Levenberg-Marquardt bundle adjustment and multi-camera ($N \ge 3$) ray intersections.

\section{Conclusion}
A numerically robust two-view 3-D measurement workstation has been presented. By integrating isotropic Hartley similarity normalization, thin Householder QR with cyclic Jacobi SVD for 11-parameter DLT calibration, guarded Brown--Conrady distortion inversion, and inhomogeneous Householder QR triangulation, the pipeline eliminates condition-number squaring without forming normal equations. Multi-tier quality gates verify conditioning, ray convergence ($\theta_{\mathrm{ray}} \ge 1.5^\circ$), and reprojection error. The zero-allocation, flat-array architecture enables real-time 60+ FPS processing, verified across dual-language test suites and test-range metrology.
